## 2. Recursive partial expansion, recurse 3-4 (item 5 REOPEN, G2)

Item 5 parked depth explicitly: render-bound until item 8. Item 8 landed
1.2-1.6x then a further 1.6-1.7x, so I re-measured before committing to a
batch rather than assuming: cost is **linear at ~17 ms/partial for a 4 s
render**, consistent across 216→5000 partials (6 cells), which also confirms
the `base·(2·count+1)^(recurse+1)` count formula. 18 patches, 16 rendered in
285 s. `patches/expand_sweep4/`, `renders/expand_sweep4/`.

### The question the batch was built to answer

Depth was worth a round only if it does something count and width don't, so
the batch holds total spread fixed at 1.0 mult and varies only that:

| pair | distance | what differs |
|---|---|---|
| `micro_r3` vs `ctrl_r1_match` | **4.36** | same partial count (648) + same spread, half the depth |
| `micro_r3` vs `ctrl_r0_wide` | 12.41 | same spread, one level, 9x fewer partials |
| `micro_r3` vs `micro_r2` | 5.68 | one level shallower |
| `micro_r4` vs `micro_r3` | 8.80 | one level deeper |
| `flat_deep` vs `peaked_deep` | 50.46 | same geometry, opposite loPct floor |
| `taper_flat_c2` vs `taper_steep_c2` | 4.96 | only `power`, at count=2 |

Read against the batch's own scale (median pair 18.98, widest 66.08):
**depth is real but second-order.** Holding count and spread fixed, halving
the depth moves the embedding 4.36 — nonzero, but the same size as a `power`
tweak (4.96) and ~4x below the median pair. The big levers in this batch are
`loPct` (50.46) and the rule-breakers. Novelty top of batch: `flat_deep` 77.1,
`supersonic_r4` 74.1, `inflate_r3` 57.2, `semitone_r3` 52.3, `pi_r4` 50.8.

My read: **stop spending rounds on depth per se.** Recurse 3-4 is affordable
now and worth having available, but it is not where the interesting sound is.

### The batch found a live trap: `power` is inert at count=1

`peaked_deep` rendered **byte-identical to `micro_r3`** (same sha256), despite
differing in `power1/power2` (1.5 vs 3.0). Cause, in `apply_expand_rule`:
side-partial amplitude is
`ampl*loPct + ampl*(1-loPct)*pow(t, power)` with `t = j/count` on the left and
`(count-1-j)/count` on the right. **At count=1 both give t=0, and `pow(0,p)=0`
for every p>0** — so the taper term vanishes and every side partial lands flat
on the `loPct` floor, whatever `power` says.

That is not a coding bug; it is a defensible reading of the formula. But it is
a trap, and it bit this batch: I used count=1 throughout to afford depth, which
silently made `power` a no-op across the whole round and turned one cell into a
duplicate. Confirmed live by the added regression pair `taper_flat_c2` /
`taper_steep_c2` — identical except `power`, at count=2, and they render
**different** (distinct sha256, distance 4.96). So the parameter works; it just
can't be reached at count=1. Queued for your call (REVIEW 4) since fixing it
changes existing expand patches; new backlog item 12.

## 3. Piano unison beating (report open item #1) — MEASURED, NOT LOCKABLE

`research/ml_ears/piano_beating.py`, new. The piano report's top open item was
"unison-string beating rates unmeasured (needed to lock shimmer dims)". The
goal was to lock `shimmerHz`/`shimmerDepth`/`shimmerCoherence` from measurement
rather than spend CMA-ES evals on them, per your clarinet-bed pattern.

**The honest answer is that the rate is not measurable this way, and I can show
that rather than assert it.** Built-in control: single-strung bass should not
beat like a trichord.

Attempt 1 failed its own control — every partial of every note "beat" at 0.35
or 0.69 Hz, which on a 3 s window is FFT bins 1 and 2. Leftover decay curvature,
not beating. Attempt 2 (8 s window, 4-cycle floor, residual high-passed at the
floor) fixed the depth control but I did not trust the rates, because they piled
up just above the new floor. So I made the suspicion testable — re-run with the
floor raised and see whether the rates follow it:

| note | floor 0.50 | floor 0.75 | floor 1.12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| C2 | 0.63 | 0.96 | 3.16 |
| G2 | 0.63 | 1.01 | 1.39 |
| C4 | 0.65 | 1.04 | 1.52 |
| C5 | 1.98 | 1.98 | 1.98 |
| C6 | 1.90 | 1.90 | 1.90 |

**9 of 11 rates climb with the floor; 2 hold.** The rate is a window artifact
except at C5/C6, which hold dead steady at ~1.9-2.0 Hz across all three floors
and are the only rates I'd believe. The script now prints this verdict itself
and refuses to recommend locking `shimmerHz`.

What DID survive, and it is structural rather than a fitted number: the
single-strung control separates cleanly on **depth**. B0/C1 (genuinely single)
come in at 0.029 / 0.016; everything multi-strung sits at 0.106-0.285, median
0.150 — a **5-9x contrast**. So per-partial envelope modulation in the unison
registers is real and worth modelling; its magnitude is order 0.1-0.2. The
data also **reclassified F1**: labelled single on my first pass, it measures
0.150, squarely in the multi-strung band. Real pianos go single-strung only for
the lowest few keys, so F1 is a bichord and the label was wrong, not the data.

`shimmerCoherence` (0.81) is computed FROM the rates, so it inherits their
unreliability and I am not proposing it as a locked value either.

Two attempts, so per the 2-attempt rule this goes back on the backlog rather
than getting a third — annotated with the method that would actually work: stop
inferring the beat from the envelope and resolve the unison strings as separate
spectral lines (they are split by the beat frequency, 0.6-2 Hz, which a ≥2 s
coherent FFT resolves directly in the bass/mid). Backlog item 13.
