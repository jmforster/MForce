## 0. The headline, and it is embarrassing

Matt's report: *"If by low C you meant C2, that does not sound for me (using
'loud' patch). Lowest sounding note for that patch is G2 (49Hz)."* The
dispatch read that as a live-vs-offline divergence and told me to find where
the two paths differ.

**They do not differ.** Rendered through `mforce_cli` and through
`mforce_ui --gencheck` (the UI's own `generate_unified` build of the same
patch), every note from midi 24 to midi 39 agrees to **0.000001** — peak,
rms, harmonic lock and cents. `--gatecheck` reports `gateable=1
envelopes=2` on the candidate. The parity table is in
`renders/dsp/audition/trombone2/measurements.json` under `parity` and the
check ships as `gen_trombone2.py parity`.

What diverged was the **note naming**, and it was mine.

- `mforce_ui` draws its keyboard from `baseNote = g_transport.octave * 12`
  (main.cpp) and labels the transport readout by the *house* convention,
  `octave = midi/12` — the comment at main.cpp says so explicitly and cites
  comp REVIEW 19.
- `gen_trombone1.py` named notes scientifically, `octave = midi/12 - 1`.

So the two ends of the conversation were **one octave apart for the whole
round**. Matt's "C2" is midi 24 = 32.70 Hz, an octave below anything attempt
1 ever tuned (its map started at midi 36). His "G2" is midi 31 = 49.00 Hz —
and 49.00 Hz is exactly what the render says too: midi 31 is the lowest note
in attempt 1 that reaches a real harmonic lock (0.63) and audible level
(rms 0.037), with midi 24–28 silent at rms 0.0001. His "G6" is midi 79 =
784 Hz, and midi 80 drops to a fifth of the level with midi 81+ producing
attack transient only — his word for that was "tink". **Every range
observation he made is reproduced to the Hz by the offline render.** His
ears were right and my labels were wrong.

`gen_trombone2.py` uses the house convention throughout — README, tables,
report — and says so at the top of the file and the top of the README.

Engine-side finding, documented and NOT acted on (zero engine code this
round): the two naming conventions coexist in the repo. The UI uses
house; `gen_trombone1.py`, `gen_trombone2.py` (now), and several other tools
pick their own. Worth a single shared helper eventually, so a report and a
keyboard can never disagree again.

## 1. Loudness (work order 1)

### The reference, measured

`patches/library/winds/oboe1.json` rendered through this pipeline:
**sounding rms 0.1761**, 0.5 s window ceiling 0.2705, peak 0.617. It is the
median of `patches/library/winds/` on both metrics — the family measures
0.5 s ceilings 0.160 / 0.199 / 0.271 / 0.510 / 0.620 and sounding rms
0.117 / 0.132 / 0.176 / 0.265 / 0.396 (flute1, clarinet1, oboe1, bassoon1,
oboe2). The number is re-measured at every run; if that render fails the
run says so rather than substituting a remembered value.

### What was actually wrong

Two things, and the second was the bigger one.

**(a) The queue was peak-normalised and never compared to anything.**
Attempt 1's cells measured, sounding rms: line 0.0957, loud 0.0929, soft
**0.0199**. Against the reference that is −5.3 dB for the line and
**−18.9 dB for `soft`** — which is exactly why "soft is barely audible at
100% speakers".

**(b) The instrument's own register was 14.3 dB out of balance.** Measured
per note at unity gain, attempt 1 runs rms 0.0429 at midi 36 and 0.1946 at
midi 72. Peak-normalising a line that crosses the whole range therefore sets
the gain from the top note and buries the bottom — which is where a trombone
lives. This is inside the patch, so it was there when he played it live too.

### The fix

A measured per-note output trim (`TRIM_MAP`, a `CurveNode` points map on the
played note feeding a multiply at the end of the chain, solved by rendering
every chromatic note and equalising the sustained rms). Trims run ×1.0 at
the loudest note to ×5.0 at the quietest. Then each cell's gain is set so
its **sounding rms matches the measured library reference**, capped so no
peak exceeds 0.90.

| | attempt 1 | attempt 2 |
|---|---|---|
| register level spread, F2..G6 | **14.3 dB** | **0.0 dB** |
| `line` sounding rms (dB vs library) | 0.0957 (−5.3) | 0.1169 (−3.6) |
| `soft` sounding rms (dB vs library) | 0.0199 (−18.9) | 0.0804 (−6.8) |
| worst cell vs library | −18.9 dB | −4.8 dB |
| 0.5 s level ceiling (gate: ≤ 0.5) | 0.188 | 0.181 |

`soft` is **+12.1 dB** on last round. The remaining 0 to −4.8 dB is the peak
cap biting on this instrument's crest factor, not a choice — `held_6s`, a
single sustained note, lands at −0.0 dB, and the multi-note lines cannot
reach the reference without peaking past 0.90.

## 2. The range (work orders 2 and 4)

Attempt 1 solved midi 36–72. Matt plays midi 31–79. Below the solved range
the maps extrapolate flat and the rendered pitch runs **+6 cents at midi 35
to +80 cents at midi 29** — midi 31, his lowest note, is **+42 cents**,
nearly a quarter tone, with the lip parked on a seed ratio instead of the
middle of its speaking window. Above the solved range it runs −1 to −6 cents
flat.

This round solves **midi 29–79 (F2–G6 in his names, 51 chromatic notes)**.

| gate | attempt 1 | attempt 2 |
|---|---|---|
| notes solved | 37 (midi 36–72) | **51 (midi 29–79)** |
| notes that speak, of those | 37 | **51** |
| worst tuning error | 4.2 c | **1.9 c** |
| tuning regressions vs attempt 1 | — | **none** |
| speak-time regressions vs attempt 1 | — | **none** |

The regression gate is explicit in the run: every note in the shared range
(midi 36–72) is checked for "stopped speaking", "tuning got worse than
±5 c", and "speak time more than 1.5× slower". All three came back empty.

### Breath support — the other half, and it is a real finding

The lip-window solver could not find a speaking window at all for midi 78
and 79. Chasing that produced the round's second solid result: **a note's
ignition threshold climbs steeply with register, and attempt 1 blew a flat
pressure at every pitch.** Measured, as a single note from silence, the
lowest blowing pressure at which the note starts:

| note | midi | ignition threshold |
|---|---|---|
| F2 … B5 | 29–71 | 8 kPa (the bottom of the scan) |
| C6 | 72 | 12 kPa |
| D6 | 74 | 16 kPa |
| D#6 | 75 | 20 kPa |
| E6 | 76 | 28 kPa |
| F6 | 77 | 36 kPa |
| F#6 | 78 | 52 kPa |
| G6 | 79 | 72 kPa |

The measurement has to be a **single note in its own render**. A note
rendered after another note inherits the bore's leftover energy and starts
on it — which is why attempt 1's top notes looked fine (they were only ever
measured in a line) and will not start on their own. So the patch now
carries a measured `PRESS_MAP`, per-note breath support parked
PRESS_MARGIN = 1.6× over each note's own threshold, **floored at 1.0** so
every note that already worked is unchanged and the regression gate can only
be moved by the notes that needed the help. In practice the map is 1.0
everywhere below midi 76 and rises to 2.5 at midi 79.

Result: F#6 and G6 go from "will not start unless another note just played"
to starting on their own, and the solved range reaches G6 at 0.2 cents.

A caveat stated plainly: G6's threshold works out to 72 kPa of normalised
mouth pressure, and a real trombonist tops out around 20–25 kPa. The model's
pressure scale is Berjamin Table 2's normalisation, not an absolute claim,
but a physical instrument does not need nine times the breath for its top
note. That is a property of this lip/bore pairing worth its own look.

Also noted as data: with the maps extended, midi 26–28 (D2–E2) now speak too
(lock 0.57–0.89) though still +12 to +36 cents sharp — the range now runs a
few semitones below the solved bottom rather than stopping dead.

## 3. Brightness (work order 3)

A voicing lowpass now lives in the candidate: **two cascaded SVF lowpasses**
(24 dB/oct) at the very end of the chain, after the bell transmission, before
the trim and output gain. `SVFSource.cutoffFreq` is a ParamDescriptor, so it
could be driven by a ValueSource; it is a constant here, for the reason
below.

Two corners are staged and **Matt's ears pick**: `line.wav` at **1100 Hz**
and `line_brighter.wav` at **2200 Hz**.

| corner | median centroid | bottom note | top note | energy > 1 kHz | level spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| off | 735 Hz | 641 | 1471 | 0.184 | 8.4 dB |
| 700 Hz | 436 | 436 | 832 | 0.005 | 4.7 dB |
| 900 Hz | 474 | 474 | 867 | 0.012 | 1.9 dB |
| **1100 Hz (candidate)** | **498** | 498 | 916 | 0.022 | **0.0 dB** |
| 1500 Hz | 560 | 528 | 1031 | 0.046 | 2.4 dB |
| **2200 Hz (alternative)** | **611** | 560 | 1196 | 0.087 | 4.9 dB |
| 3200 Hz | 652 | 588 | 1315 | 0.125 | 6.6 dB |

Read the last column carefully: it is measured **with the shipped trim map
in place**, and the trim was solved at 1100 Hz, so 0.0 dB there is by
construction and the other rows show what moving the corner costs in
register balance. Measured without any trim, the same sweep ran 16.8 dB
unfiltered down to 12.0-14.5 dB filtered — a fixed corner slightly IMPROVES
the balance, which is the opposite of what the keytracked corner did.

### One thing was built, measured, and dropped

A fixed corner is the obvious reading of "a final LPF", and I did not take
it first. Attempt 1's spectral centroid sits at 710–770 Hz at **every**
pitch — that is the bell's own passband — so as a multiple of the note being
played, brightness runs **13.4× f0 at the bottom of the range and 1.9× at
the top**. That argues for a corner that follows the note, so I built one:
a points map on the played note driving the SVF cutoff.

Measured, it works on its own terms — at 6× f0 the brightness flattens from
13.4/1.9 to 4.1/1.8, median 3.19 → 2.24 — **and it is wrong anyway**,
because it strips the low register of the 500–1000 Hz band that is the only
place those notes have any energy. The register level spread goes from
13.1 dB to **36.5 dB**, and the trim then has to put 36 dB back, which is
re-amplifying a near-sine. That is the opposite of "brass character in the
low register". Matt's own words separate the two complaints anyway
("everything too bright" AND "brass character disappears in the lower
register" — not "the bottom is too bright"). Fixed corner ships; the
keytracked experiment is recorded here and in the generator's comments.

Worth recording against the word "bright": in absolute terms this patch was
never bright. Measured whole-file on the queue WAVs, 95% of attempt 1's
`line.wav` energy is below 1571 Hz and 22.2% is above 1 kHz, against
**67.1% above 1 kHz for `renders/library/oboe_default.wav`**. (Per-note
across the range the unfiltered figure is 18.4%.) Whatever Matt is hearing
as brightness, it is not high-frequency content by that measure — which is
also why I do not claim to know what the right corner is, and why two of
them are staged.

## 4. The low register (work order 4) — two failed hypotheses, reported

The order was: characterise objectively, find measurable anomalies, fix
those, no perceptual theorising. Two hypotheses were built, measured, and
came back negative. Both failures were **metric bugs of my own**, and both
are worth recording because the metrics are reusable.

**Hypothesis 1: the sustain is non-harmonic at the bottom.** First
measurement said yes, spectacularly — residual 0.99 at midi 31 against 0.00
at midi 72. It was an artifact of a **fixed 16-harmonic comb**: at 49 Hz
sixteen harmonics only reach 784 Hz, so every real harmonic above that
counted as noise. With the comb run over the whole band to 12 kHz, the
sustain residual is **0.02–0.05 at every note in the range**. The sustain
down there is as periodic as the top. Not it.

**Hypothesis 2: the low notes take longer to become periodic than to become
loud** — i.e. level arrives fast and pitch arrives late, which is what a
rattle would be. First measurement said every note failed to lock at all,
which was a second metric bug: the comb tolerance has to be at least the
analysis window's own Hann mainlobe (2 bins), and a fixed 0.6% tolerance
makes every window shorter than ~0.5 s read as inharmonic regardless of
content. Fixed, with a window scaled in periods so a low note is judged on
the same number of cycles as a high one:

| note | midi | time to periodicity | time to 90% level |
|---|---|---|---|
| G2 | 31 | 50 ms | 80 ms |
| C3 | 36 | 10 ms | 78 ms |
| E3 | 40 | 10 ms | 64 ms |
| G3 | 43 | 45 ms | 108 ms |
| C4 | 48 | 20 ms | 72 ms |
| G4 | 55 | 45 ms | 168 ms |
| C5 | 60 | 40 ms | 106 ms |
| C6 | 72 | 90 ms | 202 ms |

The bottom locks **faster** than the top. Not it either.

Four onset levers were ablated as well (breath grain amplitude, the
initial-lip-velocity kick, the steepener, overshoot amount and duration).
Breath grain: no change to five digits. Kick at 0, 3 and 8×: no change.
Steepener off: no change at the bottom. Overshoot amount 0.75/3/5: no
change. What *does* change things is making the onset slower — a 200 ms
overshoot decay, or a 20 ms attack, **kills the bottom three notes
outright**. The sharp pressure transient is what ignites low notes, so the
prompt's own suggestion (scale the onset in periods, which would lengthen it
at the bottom) is measurably the wrong direction and was not taken.

**Two attempts, both negative — so I am calling the wall on "there is a
separate low-register onset defect".** What is measurably broken at the
notes Matt was actually playing is in §1 and §2: those notes were 42 cents
sharp, untuned, with the lip off its window, at a level 14 dB under the top
of the range. Both are fixed, and `low_before.wav` / `low_after.wav` is that
comparison on his notes, deliberately **not** level-matched — `low_before`
is levelled exactly the way last round's queue was, so the pair carries the
real loudness difference (−10.3 dB vs −3.5 dB against the library
reference). If the rattle survives that, it is a third thing and his ears
are the only instrument that can say so.

## 5. Loudness–brightness linkage (backlog 74a, work order 5)

Re-measured at real level. **Still the weak spot, and still small.**

| | soft (31 kPa) | loud (50 kPa) |
|---|---|---|
| spectral centroid | 473 Hz | 477 Hz |
| energy above 1 kHz | 0.0194 | 0.0210 |
| sounding rms | 0.0804 | 0.1253 |

Attempt 1 measured 742 → 751 Hz over a nominally wider pressure range; this
is the same non-result at a level where it can finally be judged. The gate
passes on sign alone and should not be read as a win.

**Matt's own data point is now explained.** He wrote: *"high C in 'soft'
struggles to lock in while 'loud' locks much quicker."* That is the ignition
threshold in §2. Attempt 1 blew one flat pressure at every pitch, and its
soft cell sat at S 0.45 — below midi 72's measured 0.6 threshold. The note
was under the pressure it needs to start at all, so it crawled in on
whatever the tube happened to give it. With the breath map both settings are
above threshold at every note.

The cost is worth stating: **this instrument's soft end is limited by its top
note.** The full audition line only speaks from S 1.50 upward, so `soft` is
31 kPa and `loud` 50 kPa — a 1.6× range, narrower than attempt 1's claimed
4×. Attempt 1's wider window was measured on a *line*, where each note
inherits the previous note's energy; measured note-by-note from silence it
is not there. Direct speak-time measurement across the soft/loud pair is
noisy on single top notes (C6: 218 ms soft vs 180 ms loud; G6: 14 vs 458)
and is **not** offered as evidence either way.

## 6. What shipped

Candidate: `patches/audition/trombone1/trombone_attempt2.json`, 43 nodes,
instrument-style with the `ui.noteFaces` keyboard face, `gateable=1`,
sitting beside attempt 1 for A/B.

Three measured maps carried in the patch, all `CurveNode` points maps on the
played note (never taps — attempt 1's wiring trap still applies):
`TUNE_MAP` (lip ratio, widest speaking window), `BORE_MAP` (air-column trim
for equal temperament), `PRESS_MAP` (breath support), `TRIM_MAP` (register
level). Plus the voicing lowpass, `Voice0`/`Voice1`, at 1100 Hz.

Ears queue, 8 cells in `renders/dsp/audition/trombone2/`:

| cell | what it is |
|---|---|
| `line` | the candidate, G2–G6, darker voicing (1100 Hz) |
| `line_brighter` | same take, 2200 Hz — **the A/B that matters** |
| `low_after` | F2–E3 this round |
| `low_before` | same notes, attempt 1's tuning AND attempt 1's levelling |
| `soft` / `loud` | 31 vs 50 kPa, one shared gain |
| `short_notes` | articulation at real level |
| `held_6s` | sustain and release |

All gates passed: 51/51 notes speak, worst tuning 1.9 c, no regression
against attempt 1, every cell under the 0.5 level ceiling (worst 0.181), no
clipping, every staged A/B audible, candidate key-gateable, live/offline
parity 0.000001.

## 7. Open, and not in this build

- **Loudness–brightness (74a).** Unchanged verdict: measurable, far too
  small. The two suspects named in attempt 1's report (the bell reflection
  throwing away the harmonics harder blowing makes; the steepener sitting
  outside a loop whose HF is now bell-limited) are still untested.
- **G6 needs 72 kPa.** Physically wrong even allowing for the normalisation.
  A property of the lip/bore pairing, not of the maps.
- **Note naming has no single source of truth in the repo.** Documented in
  §0; no engine or tool-wide change made this round.
- Everything attempt 1's §9 listed is still true: the slide is not a slide,
  the bore is a lumped loop rather than a waveguide, no viscothermal loss on
  the slide, minimum-phase-fitted bell magnitude only, non-inverting loop
  sign, and nothing anywhere is fitted to a real trombone recording.
