## 1. It was never the physics — it was two of my own gain stages

### (a) The nonlinearity was being fed a level-corrected wave (work order 2)

`gen_trombone2.calibrate()` ran three passes, and the first one set

```
in_gain  = DRIVE_PEAK / peak(cell)      on NL_in, the steepener's input
out_gain = 1 / in_gain                  after it
```

**per cell**. So attempt 2's `soft` cell and its `loud` cell both drove the
steepener to a peak of exactly 0.800, and both came out with exactly the
same distortion. The one component in the instrument whose job is "brighter
when louder" had the loudness removed before it ever saw the wave.

Measured, with the normalisation off (`gen_trombone3.py drive`, midi 46):

| blowing pressure S | raw bore peak | attempt 2's NL_in peak | attempt 3's |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.20 | 0.0729 | 0.800 | 0.0729 |
| 0.50 | 0.1791 | 0.800 | 0.1791 |
| 1.00 | 0.3607 | 0.800 | 0.3607 |
| 2.00 | 0.7266 | 0.800 | 0.7266 |
| 3.00 | 1.0896 | 0.800 | 1.0896 |

and what the steepener then contributes, ablated at depth 0 vs 0.03:

| S | centroid, steepener off | on | Δ | >1 kHz off | on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.20 | 425 | 431 | **+6** | 0.0121 | 0.0129 |
| 0.50 | 440 | 455 | +15 | 0.0162 | 0.0199 |
| 1.00 | 449 | 480 | +31 | 0.0192 | 0.0296 |
| 2.00 | 451 | 503 | +52 | 0.0199 | 0.0396 |
| 3.00 | 446 | 511 | **+65** | 0.0184 | 0.0430 |

It is a dynamics component the moment it is allowed to see dynamics. Fixed:
`DRIVE_GAIN = 1.0`, a constant, so NL_in carries the air column's own
travelling wave at its own amplitude. Measured drive span pp → ff: **23 dB
on A#3, 28 dB on F5.**

One consequence had to be handled and is not a fudge: with real drive, F5
at ff reaches 3.19 into a steepener curve whose knots the probe module put
at ±2 (never tested, because attempt 2's normalisation capped the drive at
0.8). The knots now go to ±4 at the **identical slope** — inside ±2 the
curve is unchanged to the digit, outside it the published law still applies
instead of flattening. It is gated: no note's ff drive may leave the domain
(worst measured 3.19).

### (b) The whole instrument was already playing at forte (work order 3)

Attempt 2's soft/loud were 31 and 50 kPa — a 1.6× window — because ONE
global pressure had to keep G6 alive, and G6's ignition threshold is 72 kPa.
Measured note by note from silence (`span`, 51 notes × 19 pressures, single
note per render), A#3 speaks in tune from **2.7 kPa to 53 kPa, 20:1**, and
attempt 2 sat at 40 kPa, near the top of it. There was no softer to go to,
for any note, because the top note owned the setting.

Per-register spans achieved, pp → ff, in the model's normalised kPa:

| note | pp | mf (unchanged from attempt 2) | ff | pp → ff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F2 | 26.6 | 40.0 | 101.1 | 3.8:1 |
| G2 | 8.7 | 40.0 | 82.8 | 9.5:1 |
| C4 | 3.3 | 40.0 | 45.4 | 13.7:1 |
| A#3 | 3.3 | 40.0 | 45.4 | 13.7:1 |
| F5 | 4.8 | 40.0 | 105.2 | 22:1 |
| C6 | 14.9 | 40.0 | 150.8 | 10:1 |
| G6 | 91.6 | 100.0 | 206.9 | 2.3:1 |

Read the ends: **G6's pp is louder than most of the instrument's ff**, and
F#2/G6 have essentially no dynamic range. That is this lip/bore pairing, not
the mapping.

### The mechanism, and why velocity

Pressure is now `P(note, v) = P_mf(note) · (1 + Vdn(v)·Wdn(note) +
Vup(v)·Wup(note))` — two velocity curves (one for each side of mf) times two
per-note weight maps solved from that note's own measured floor and ceiling.
Two curves rather than one because a note's floor and its ceiling are not
the same distance from mf, and a single global multiplier is exactly what
pinned attempt 2.

At velocity 0.8 — the score default AND the UI's default — both terms are
zero and the pressure is **exactly attempt 2's**, which is why the
regression gates hold by construction rather than by luck.

Verified end to end: rendering midi 48 at velocity 0.25 and rendering it at
the equivalent fixed `scale` give identical cents (−1.9), lock (1.00) and
rms (0.00223). And the sustained mouth pressure matches the closed form
`S·√(f_lip/F_REF)` to four digits at every velocity tested.

### The velocity fader, named rather than hidden

`Instrument::prepare_voice_at` mixes a voice at `velocity * (1+boost) *
volume` (engine/include/mforce/render/instrument.h:295). Measured with a
patch that ignores velocity, output rms is exactly proportional to it
(0.2 → 0.02931, 0.8 → 0.11726). Velocity is now the breath, and the breath
already changes the level through the physics, so that fader would count the
same dynamic twice. `Vcomp` divides it back out — **at the very output**,
unity at velocity 0.8.

## 2. Levelling (work order 1)

Matt's directive: *"if I caused kludgy level-tweaking to be added due to my
'really soft' comments let's remove that... we want the sound to be right,
can always get bigger speakers."*

### (a) How much of the 14.3 dB was artifact

Decomposed (`gen_trombone3.py trim`, degree-2 fit in dB against midi):

| | |
|---|---|
| attempt 2's applied trim, range | 14.0 dB |
| smooth register ramp inside it | **15.1 dB** |
| note-to-note scatter | **±4.9 dB** (rms 1.15) |
| biggest jump between ADJACENT semitones, applied trim | **5.0 dB** |
| ...in the smooth ramp alone | 0.40 dB |

Those are two different things and they get two different answers. A 5 dB
jump between two neighbouring semitones is unambiguously model artifact (a
partial-crossing accident) and **the scatter correction is kept in full**. A
smooth ramp where the top of the range is stronger than the bottom is at
least partly real — a bell radiates high frequencies more efficiently than
low ones — so **65% of the ramp is applied and 35% is left in the sound**.

Measured result: register level spread **0.0 dB (attempt 2) → 5.3 dB**, top
louder. Not flat, not 14 dB. If Matt wants either end instead it is one
constant (`TRIM_SLOPE_KEEP`).

### (b) Where it sits, measured rather than asserted

The trim is the last thing before the output gain, after the steepener and
after the bell transmission. Verified by rendering the steepener's input
node with the trim on and off:

```
trim on   NL_in peak 0.726624  rms 0.314808
trim off  NL_in peak 0.726624  rms 0.314808
```

Identical to six decimal places. The trim cannot change what any
nonlinearity sees.

### (c) What was removed outright

Attempt 2's `calibrate()` had three passes; two of them are gone. What
survives is one output gain per queue WAV that matches the cell's sounding
rms to `patches/library/winds/oboe1.json` (re-measured this run: 0.1761),
capped at peak 0.90 — presentation only, exactly the run contract's
loudness calibration, and it multiplies the finished signal.

## 3. Lip closure (work order 4) — measured, and nothing needed changing

The published mechanism (JASA'96, `docs/research/nonlinear_bore/
MSALLAM_DIGEST.md` §2/§6) is that the lip closing harder at forte steepens
the mouthpiece pressure pulse, and rise rate is the severity parameter. The
dispatch's suspicion was that our lip might never reach its clamp, leaving
the source smooth at every dynamic. Measured from the graph's own `Lip`
(displacement, mm from equilibrium; the valve shuts below −0.50) and `Pe`
(mouthpiece pressure, ×20 kPa):

| note | closed at pp | closed at ff | rise rate pp | rise rate ff | change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F2 | 28% | 30% | 8 873 /s | 75 753 /s | 9× |
| A#3 | 28% | 31% | 837 /s | 23 312 /s | **28×** |
| F5 | 24% | 38% | 837 /s | 44 657 /s | **53×** |

The lip is shut for roughly a quarter of every cycle even at pp and the
rise rate goes up by up to 53× across the range. **No lip parameter was
touched** — one measurement, negative for the hypothesis, and the work order
closes.

### An engine trap, logged and NOT fixed

At ff the lip displacement sits on the engine's global ±8 value clamp
(`engine/include/mforce/core/dsp_value_source.h:144`, *"clamped to ±8 — so
a runaway loop saturates"*) for **53–69% of each cycle**. The negative rail
is harmless, because the valve is shut below −0.5 anyway. The positive rail
is not: flow is proportional to the opening, so the top of this
instrument's dynamic range is being squared off by a safety clamp rather
than by its own physics. Zero engine code this round; this is a backlog
item, and it is a candidate explanation for why ff stops getting brighter
above a point on some notes.

### A measurement trap worth keeping

A probe that renders an internal node is multiplied by the voice mix gain
(`velocity × volume`) exactly like the real output. The first pass of this
round read a soft note's lip as *never closing at all* because of it. Caught
by checking the probe against the closed form for `Pm`: measured/closed-form
came out 0.1746 / 0.5599 / 0.7000 at velocities 0.25 / 0.80 / 1.00 —
velocity × 0.7 to four digits. `probe_signal()` divides both gains out and
says so.

## 5. Where it is still flat, honestly

F2 moves +1.3%. Its measured span is 3.8:1 against A#3's 13.7:1, and its pp
had to be walked up twice: the solver's first answer (20 kPa) fell into a
narrow pressure hole the 1.35× scan grid stepped over, and the second
(23 kPa) ignited on a 1.0 s note but not a 1.2 s one, sitting on the hole's
edge at +14 to +31 cents. 26.6 kPa is clean at every note length tested.
So the bottom of this instrument has 3.5 dB of breath between "as soft as it
goes" and normal, and it sounds like it.

**Work order 5 (steepener depth riding the breath envelope) was NOT built.**
The escalation was conditioned on 1–4 leaving brightness flat, and they did
not. The signal path stays honest.

headroom; the cell is levelled as one so its internal dynamics survive.

## 8. Open, and not in this build

- **The low register's dynamic range** (F2 3.8:1). Not a mapping problem —
  the note goes out of tune below 26 kPa and falls into a pressure hole at
  20 kPa.
- **The engine's ±8 clamp is inside the lip's operating range at ff.**
  Needs an engine round, or a rescaling of the lip's normalised units so ff
  fits under the rail.
- **G6 still needs 92 kPa for its pp** — backlog 74b unchanged.
- Everything in attempt 1 §9 and attempt 2 §7 stays true: the slide is not a
  slide, the bore is a lumped loop rather than a waveguide, no viscothermal
  loss, minimum-phase-fitted bell magnitude, non-inverting loop sign, and
  nothing anywhere is fitted to a real trombone recording.
