## 1. Smyth & Scott — what was fetched, what was transcribed, what was not

**Fetched this run.** `curl` on
`https://asmp-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/counter/pdf/10.1155/2011/151436.pdf`
returned the full open-access paper: Tamara Smyth and Frederick S. Scott,
"Trombone Synthesis by Model and Measurement", EURASIP Journal on Advances in
Signal Processing 2011:151436, 13 pages, 2 481 426 bytes, CC-BY. Text
extracted from the PDF's own text layer. The SFU link in the digest
(`summit.sfu.ca/item/11130`) was not needed.

**Transcribed verbatim (§4.2, Tables 1–2, Eqs. 21–27):**

```
(21)   a(x) = b (x + x0)^(-gamma)          the bell profile

(22)   [p+_n ; p-_n] = A_n [p+_{n+1} ; p-_{n+1}]
(23)   A_n = the 2x2 conical scattering matrix in Z_n, Z*_n, e^{±jkL_{n+1}}
(24)   [p+_1 ; p-_1] = A_1 A_2 ... A_{N-1} [p+_N ; p-_N]
(25)   P = prod_{n=1}^{N-1} A_n

(26)   R_B = p-_1 / p+_1 = lambda^2(w) (P21 + P22 RL(w)) / (P11 + P12 RL(w))

(27)   T_B(w) = p+_N lambda(w) TL(w) / p+_1 = lambda(w) TL(w)
                                              / (P11 + P12 RL(w))
```

**Table 1 — "Parameters for Bessel horn described by (21) best fitting the
trombone bell":**

| quantity | variable | value |
|---|---|---|
| Length of the bell (m) | | .502 |
| Radius at bell mouth (m) | | .108 |
| Radius at small end (m) | | .01 |
| Bell flare constant | gamma | .7 |
| Position of the bell mouth (m) | x0 | .0174 |
| Fitting parameter | b | 0.0063 |

**Table 2 — trombone tubular sections** (length cm / radius cm): t. inner
slide 70.8 / 0.69; t. outer slide extended 53 / 0.72; slide crook 17.7 /
0.74; b. outer slide extended 53 / 0.72; b. inner slide 71.1 / 0.69;
gooseneck 24.1 / 0.71; tuning slide 25.4 / 0.75, 1.07; bell flare 56.7 / 1,
10.8. Prose from the same section: the bell is modelled with **eight** conical
sections; `RL`/`TL` come from Levine & Schwinger via "suitable expressions
found in [12]"; the paper imposes **minimum phase** on every filter it fits,
stating that a magnitude match then yields a phase match.

**Table 1 checks out against itself** — a useful sign the transcription is
right rather than plausible. Evaluating Eq. 21 with those six numbers:
`a(0) = 0.10739 m` against the stated mouth radius 0.108, and
`a(0.502) = 0.00997 m` against the stated small-end radius 0.010.

**NOT obtained, stated plainly:**

1. **No numeric filter, anywhere in the paper.** Figures 8, 13 and 16 are
   raster plots of the measured and modelled reflection/transmission
   magnitudes with no data table behind them. **Nothing in this round is
   traced off a curve, and no coefficient here is a fabrication.**
2. **Reference [12]** (Smyth & Abel, Acta Acustica 95(6):1093–1103, 2009),
   which carries the `RL`/`TL` expressions, is paywalled and was not fetched.
3. The measured impulse responses themselves are not published as data.

**So what shipped is the paper's own model solved on the paper's own measured
bell geometry**, with three substitutions, all labelled in
`tools/bell_smyth.py` and all measured rather than asserted:

**SUB 1 — method.** Instead of Eqs. 22–25's conical traveling-wave scattering
matrices I cascade the standard pressure/volume-velocity transfer matrices of
many short *cylinders* along the same Bessel profile, terminate with a
radiation impedance, and take `R_B = (Zin − Zc)/(Zin + Zc)`. The paper itself
names piecewise-cylindrical as an accepted alternative for this job; both
converge to the Webster solution. Convergence measured, versus a 2000-slice
reference:

| slices | max abs difference in the reflection magnitude |
|---|---|
| 8 | 22.60 dB |
| 50 | 13.20 dB |
| 400 | **0.27 dB** |
| 2000 | 0.00 dB |

400 slices ship. (The 8-slice row is not a criticism of the paper — eight
*conical* frusta follow a flare far better than eight cylinders do.)

**SUB 2 — termination.** With [12] unreachable, `RL`/`TL` are textbook rather
than the paper's citation. Two were implemented and both are reported: the
exact flanged-piston (baffled) impedance
`Z/Zc = 1 − 2J1(2ka)/(2ka) + j·2H1(2ka)/(2ka)` and the Levine & Schwinger
low-frequency limit `Z/Zc = (ka)²/4 + j·0.6133·ka`. Across 30 Hz–12 kHz the
two give a **median 0.42 dB** difference in the bell reflection (max 23 dB,
and that max is up at 3–10 kHz where the reflection is already 12–22 dB down
and the flare, not the termination, sets the answer). The flanged form ships.

Wall losses use the Kirchhoff thin-boundary-layer attenuation
`alpha = (1/a)·sqrt(pi f nu)/c·(1 + (gamma−1)/sqrt(Pr))`, which reproduces
Benade's rule of thumb `2.96e−5 sqrt(f)/a` to three digits (0.09333 vs
0.09360 np/m at 1 kHz, a = 1 cm) — checked in `bell_smyth.main()`.

**SUB 3 — mic in the bell, or listener in the room.** The paper's `TL` is a
mic 7 cm off the bell, on axis, and the far-field on-axis pressure has the
same shape (`p ∝ jωρU/2πr`): it keeps rising all the way to the top of the
band. Shipped as the radiated path, that measured **−61 dB at 50 Hz relative
to 10 kHz** and the rendered line came out with a spectral centroid of
**2150 Hz** — a third of its energy in 1–2 kHz and 15 % above 4 kHz. That is
not a modelling error, it is what a microphone inside a trombone bell hears.
It is not what a listener hears. So the shipped transmission is the total
**radiated power** response, `|U|·sqrt(Re{Z_rad})`, which has the identical
+6 dB/oct rise while `ka < 1` and then goes FLAT, because above `ka ≈ 1` a
piston radiates all the power it is given and only the directivity keeps
narrowing. Same model, same geometry, different (and for a room, correct)
reading of it. Measured centroid with the power reading: **760 Hz**. Both are
in the ears queue — `line.wav` (power/room) against `line_bell_onaxis.wav`
(the paper's own on-axis reading) — so the choice is Matt's, not mine.

## 2. The change that was not on the list, and mattered most

Chasing defect B (low notes take forever) found the real cause, and it was not
the exciter.

Round 68's bore was **three note-periods long for every note**. That is an air
column that grows with the note: at C2 it is 15.7 m. A real trombone is one
tube of about 2.7 m across the whole range — the player selects which
*partial* of that tube speaks with the lip, and trims the length with the
slide. Round 68's own measurements already said the lip could do the
selecting (ratio 0.8 → mode 3, ratio 1.2 → mode 4); nothing had asked it to.

So the delay's `ratio` pin now carries the **partial number**
`p = round(f0 / 58.27 Hz)` (Bb1, a tenor trombone's own first-position
fundamental), clamped to 1–10, as a stepped `CurveNode` map on the played
note. The air column is then `f0/p`, which stays within a couple of semitones
of a real instrument's fundamental for every note in the range, and the lip
has to pick partial `p` out of a set that gets denser as `p` rises. Measured
partial assignment across the round's range:

| note | f0 (Hz) | partial | air column (Hz) |
|---|---|---|---|
| C2 | 65.41 | 1 | 65.41 |
| C3 | 130.81 | 2 | 65.41 |
| C4 | 261.63 | 4 | 65.41 |
| C5 | 523.25 | 9 | 58.14 |

This is a bore change, not a lip change — the exciter is still Berjamin's,
constant for constant.

## 3. Intonation (defect A) — it was the bore, not the lip

Round 68 recorded +18…+33 cents sharp, widening with register, and proposed
trimming it with the lip ratio ("the sweep says ~0.77 centres it"). Measured
this round, **that proposal was wrong and the measurement says why.**

1. Near the operating point the lip ratio moves the played pitch by only
   ~170 cents per unit, while the window in which the note speaks at all is
   about 0.10 wide. Chasing the last 15 cents with the lip walks off the edge
   of the window: the round's first solver oscillated between +16 cents and
   silence at C5, five renders running.
2. The residual sharpness is **+1 c at C2, +6 c at C3, +14 c at C4, +17 c at
   C5** — it grows with *frequency*, which is the signature of the delay
   line's linear-interpolated fractional read being slightly short, not of
   anything the lip is doing.

So the round solves two separate maps, both `CurveNode` point maps on the
played note, both measured at every chromatic step from C2 to C5:

- **Lip map** — the ratio is chosen for the *widest speaking window*, not for
  pitch. Scan the ratio in 0.015 steps across ±0.12 around a seed, keep every
  setting that speaks the written note, take the middle of the widest
  unbroken run. That maximizes the margin against cracking, which is the
  lip's actual job. Windows came out 7–12 steps (0.10–0.18) wide; three notes
  (A#2, A#4, B4) found windows of only 3–5 steps and are the round's thinnest
  margins.
- **Air-column map** — with the lip parked mid-window, stretch the delay until
  the rendered pitch is equal temperament. A 1 % longer delay flattens by
  ~17 cents, so this is a near-exact linear relation and the secant converges
  in 2–3 renders per note. The solved multipliers run 1.000 at C2 to 1.019 at
  C4, smooth apart from one outlier (A#2, 1.027, the same note with a thin
  lip window).

Both maps are pasted into `gen_trombone1.py` as `TUNE_MAP` and `BORE_MAP`, so
the shipped patch carries the measurement rather than a rule.

### A wiring trap, caught by measurement

First attempt wired the pole radius and the blowing-pressure keytrack off the
tuned lip frequency through a `{"tap": "Flip"}` edge, since both are functions
of `f_lip`. **That is wrong here**: a tap never advances its target, and
nothing else in the graph advances `Flip` on the sample those pins are pulled,
so both read a stale value. Cost, measured at C2: **−33.7 cents and rms
0.0023** through the tap against **+1.2 cents and rms 0.0128** wired
correctly — 35 cents flat and 15 dB quiet. Fix: each is a function of the
played note alone, so each gets its own point map on `__perf_f` and the
ordering hazard disappears. Worth remembering: the round-68 report's own
node-ordering discipline covers `{"ref"}` duplication; this is the *other*
half of the same rule and it has no gate of its own.

## 4. Attack (defect B) — the levers, and what each one was worth

Three levers, all from the model's own vocabulary; none is a new mechanism.

1. **The paper's initial condition.** Berjamin Table 2 sets `y'(0) = y1 =
   −4 m/s`. A pull graph cannot set filter state, but an impulse of momentum
   at t = 0 *is* that initial condition. The two-pole section maps a force
   sample `u` to a displacement increment `B0·u` in one sample, so one sample
   changes `y'` by `SR·B0·u·Y_REF`; setting that equal to `y1` gives
   `u_imp = y1/(SR·B0·Y_REF) = −17.088` in normalized force units. That is a
   closed form of Table 2, not a knob. It ships as a one-sample envelope
   summed into the lip force.
2. **Blowing-pressure overshoot** — what tonguing is. The Mouth envelope rises
   to 2.5× the sustain level in 6 ms and decays onto it over 50 ms.
3. **Onset breath burst** into the bore, at the same injection point as the
   steady breath grain (coupled to the tonal path, never summed onto the
   output). **Measured: no effect at all**, at 12× and again at 40× the
   steady grain, on any note, to the resolution of the speak-time metric.
   It is wired and it is **off** in the candidate. Reported rather than
   shipped.

Speak time = milliseconds from note-on to 90 % of that note's own sustained
level, measured on the radiated signal (what Matt hears), 2 ms hop / 8 ms
window. The ablation below was run on 1.2 s notes; §7's register survey uses
1.4 s notes, so the two tables differ by a few ms on the same configuration —
compare within a table, not across.

| configuration | C2 | C3 | C4 | C5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **round 68's chassis, reproduced** | **dead** (+1852 c) | **694** | **348** | **426** |
| this round, nothing but the new bore | dead | 180 | 140 | 374 |
| + lip flick, no tongue | dead | 144 | 110 | 252 |
| + tongue, no lip flick | 78 | 94 | 110 | 276 |
| **shipped (both)** | **80** | **72** | **114** | **218** |
| shipped + breath burst | 80 | 72 | 114 | 218 |
| shipped + breath burst at 40× | 80 | 72 | 114 | 220 |

The overshoot is load-bearing at the bottom: without it C2 does not speak at
all. The target was C3 under 150 ms (72 ms) and C2 workable (80 ms). C5 at
218 ms is the round's slowest note and is the honest weak spot — partial 9
needs many round trips to settle.

## 5. Register balance — a fifth defect, found by measurement

Round 68 keytracked the blowing pressure as `pm = S·PM_TAB·(f_lip/F_REF)²`.
The exponent 2 holds the static lip opening `A·pm/k` constant across the
register, since `k = m(2π f_lip)²`. Measured consequence at C2: absolute
pressure falls to ~0.5 kPa, the flow never leaves its near-linear region, and
C2 comes out as an almost pure sine **30 dB below C5** — spectral centroid
81 Hz against C5's 594 Hz.

A real player does not cut mouth pressure 40× to drop an octave; they loosen
the embouchure, i.e. the static opening *grows* toward the bottom. That is an
exponent below 2. Measured, on the round-68 bore, note rms at S = 1:

| exponent | C2 | C3 | C4 | C5 | spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 (round 68) | 0.0053 | 0.0199 | 0.0655 | 0.1676 | 30 dB |
| 1.5 | 0.0150 | 0.0404 | 0.0954 | 0.1734 | 21 dB |
| 1.0 | 0.0490 | 0.0816 | 0.1383 | 0.1794 | 11 dB |
| **0.5 (shipped)** | **0.1256** | **0.1624** | **0.1999** | **0.1855** | **4 dB** |

`PM_EXP = 0.5` ships.

## 6. Key gating (defect C) and release (defect D)

**Cause of defect C, found.** Round 68's Mouth envelope had three fractional
stages and **no expand stage** (`percent == 0`). `Envelope::gate_release()`
jumps to the stage after the expand; with no expand there is nothing to hold
in, so the envelope simply ran its own fractional schedule regardless of the
key. Not a missing feature — a missing stage.

The shipped envelope is literal-seconds (`timeMode: "seconds"`) with a real
expand in the middle:

```
0  attack        0 -> 2.5        6 ms    Expo p0.7
1  overshoot   2.5 -> 1.0       50 ms    Expo p2
2  HOLD        1.0 -> 1.0    percent 0 = expand   <- the key is held here
3  release     1.0 -> 0.0       70 ms    Expo p3
```

Offline the expand absorbs the note length, so attack and release are fixed
and only the hold grows. Live (`gated_`) the expand holds until key-up and
`gate_release()` fires stage 3. The patch is instrument-style with a
`ui.noteFaces` entry mapping `frequency` to `__perf_f`, so the UI keyboard
drives it.

**Defect D**: the release is 70 ms of Expo power 3 — it has dropped 87 % of
the way in the first 35 ms — against round 68's mirrored Sine over 14 % of
the note (224 ms on a 1.6 s note, and *longer* on a longer note, which is
what made it read as "b-waa-OO"). What is left after 70 ms is the bore's own
ring-out inside the engine's `kVoiceTailSec` (0.4 s), which is what a brass
release actually is. Both are in the ears queue as an A/B on the same line.

### The one measurement that disappoints

**Dynamics barely change the timbre.** Soft (11.1 kPa) to loud (48.9 kPa) —
more than a 4× pressure range, the two ends of the measured window — moves the
spectral centroid from **742 Hz to 751 Hz** and the energy above 1 kHz from
0.211 to 0.213. Round 68 measured 2.1× more energy above 1 kHz over a
narrower pressure range. The gate passes on sign alone and it should not be
read as a win. A real trombone opens up enormously between p and ff, and this
one does not. Two suspects, neither tested this round: the new bore is short
and its reflection filter is measured and lossy above 700 Hz, so the harmonics
the harder blowing generates are being thrown away inside the loop rather than
radiated; and the steepener, which was the round-66 brightening mechanism, is
outside a loop whose HF is now bell-limited. That is the first thing to
measure next.

## 9. What is still not in this build

- **The slide is not a slide.** The air column is `f0/p` with `p` an integer
  chosen by rounding, so the "slide position" is whatever that implies rather
  than one of seven real positions, and the instrument has no notion of a
  position being unreachable. A real tenor trombone cannot play C2 at all
  (it sits between the pedal Bb1 and E2); this one can.
- **The bore is still a lumped loop, not a waveguide.** One delay carries the
  round trip, so `p⁻` is the round-trip-filtered `p⁺` rather than an
  independently propagated counter-wave. Unchanged from round 68.
- **No viscothermal loss filter on the slide.** The bell reflection is now
  measured, but the ~2.7 m of cylindrical tube in front of it contributes no
  `√ω` wall loss of its own.
- **The reflection is minimum-phase-fitted magnitude only**, which is what
  Smyth & Scott prescribe for their own filters, but it is not the measured
  phase.
- **The loop sign convention is unchanged** from round 68 (non-inverting).
  A real brass instrument's bell reflection inverts at low frequency; adopting
  that would change which partials the tube supports and is a second change
  on top of the bore rebuild, so it was not taken this round.
- **Nothing is fitted to a real trombone recording.** Every number here comes
  from published geometry and published lip parameters. An ML-ears style
  comparison against a real trombone (the viola pipeline's machinery) is the
  obvious next measurement and is not in this round.
