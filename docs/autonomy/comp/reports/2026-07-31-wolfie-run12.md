## Front 1 — per-corpus scorer anchors [metric] ✓

Closes the run-10 caveat and the remainder of backlog #4. `corpus_baseline.py`
builds `corpus_stats_<name>.json` from each corpus's own parse path (semitone
melodies, identical feature code); `score_generated.py` and `bake_off.py`
both take `--corpus`.

Two measurement artifacts found on the way, both invalidating earlier numbers:

- **Length.** MTD is a theme corpus (median 18 notes); Nottingham and Essen
  are whole-tune (162 / 62). Whole-tune `rep_p10` came out 50 vs MTD's 6, so
  a 20-note generated phrase failed the repetition screen by construction.
  Long melodies are now windowed to theme length before aggregation
  (`rep_p10` 50 → 9).
- **Sampling.** Essen's tree is `africa/ america/ asia/ europa/`, so an
  alphabetical feed truncated at 4,000 segments anchored "folk" on 2,246
  **Chinese** tunes. Feeds are now shuffled with a fixed seed. mtd-vs-essen
  interval JSD **0.128 → 0.036**; the Essen-anchored bake-off floor
  **0.089 → 0.016**. Run 10's "Essen int_jsd floor is elevated, scoring ears
  are MTD-anchored" was this artifact, not a corpus property.

Anchors now available (windowed, seeded):

|  | mtd | nottingham | essen | essen_europa | essen_asia |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| segments | 1200 | 4000 | 4000 | 4000 | 4000 |
| rep_p10 | 6 | 8 | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| big_leap_p90 | .143 | .118 | .091 | .077 | .118 |
| selfsim_p95 | .769 | .588 | .706 | .706 | .706 |

`essen_asia` is kept as its own anchor because it is a different tradition,
not noise: **1.8%** semitone motion vs 15–24% everywhere else, 22.6% minor
thirds vs 10–14% — anhemitonic pentatonic.

Bake-off re-run under proper anchors (k=60): the run-10 conclusion **holds**
— backoff n-grams and the neural LM sit in a ~0.02 top cluster, order flips
by corpus, add-k and uniform lose everywhere. Best comp_mean: mtd ngram1
0.879, nottingham ngram3 0.887, essen ngram3 0.889 / neural 0.888.

**Structural finding (new).** Training on Essen and scoring against the
essen_asia anchor is no better than training on MTD (int_jsd 0.087 vs
0.081). The (scale-step, pulse) tokenization plus C-major realization erases
pentatonicism, so no training corpus can move the generators toward a
non-diatonic anchor. That is a representation ceiling, not a data problem —
worth knowing before anyone tries to "train on X to get X's flavor".

## Front 2 — corpus-flavored phrase batches [metric + listen] ✓

`markov_phrase --v3 --corpus <name>` trains the v3 phrase builder on any
ingested corpus and scores against that corpus's anchor. Structure rolls
(pattern / contour / transform) moved to their own RNG stream, so the same
`--seed` gives an **identical structural sequence** across corpora and the
batches differ only in figure content — a controlled A/B.

36 WAVs: `renders/corpus_flavors/{mtd,nottingham,essen}/` (12 each, scores.csv).

Cross-anchor matrix (mean composite / pooled int_jsd):

| batch \ anchor | mtd | nottingham | essen | essen_europa | essen_asia |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mtd | .818/.064 | .770/.073 | .808/.046 | .811/.036 | .804/.126 |
| nottingham | .854/.049 | .843/.051 | .845/.035 | .847/.026 | .844/.112 |
| essen | .835/.079 | .799/.078 | **.846**/.052 | .842/.043 | .822/.127 |

Corpus provenance is only weakly recoverable: mtd and essen batches do score
highest under their own anchors, Nottingham's does not, and **every** batch
minimizes int_jsd under essen_europa. The structural layer (patterns,
contours, transforms, anchoring) dominates the corpus's own transition
statistics. Where corpus choice IS visible is rhythm: 15.2 notes/phrase for
MTD vs 18.0 / 18.2 for the folk corpora, with folk favoring eighth-note
motion. Whether any of it is audible is Matt's call — REVIEW item 2.

## Front 3 — passage strategies (#6, G2) [build] ✓ prototype, spec'd for C++

`passage_strategies.py` prototypes all four passage types Matt asked for as
authored engine templates and renders them through `mforce_cli --compose` —
same route markov_phrase used before any C++ landed. 15 passages in
`renders/passage_strategies/` + scores.csv.

| strategy | design that worked | measured (mean of 3) |
|---|---|---|
| pedal_buildup | levels of equal span, doubling density, +3rd per level, held arrival, alternate restatements rotated | accel 4–8×, selfsim 0.63, rep 11.3 |
| wandering | 5–7 distinct cursor-continuous figures, register pull at ±5 degrees | selfsim 0.27, composite 0.944 |
| connective | antecedent tail sequenced 3× to a target degree | lands on target 3/3, composite 0.873 |
| fifths_sequence | −4/+3 alternating anchors = I-IV-vii-iii-vi-ii-V-I | 8 entries, composite 0.859 |
| **suite** | all four chained, one section each, pedal carried only under its own section | 82–130 notes, 37–49 beats, composite 0.885 |

Two fixes were driven by measurement, not by ear: the buildup's acceleration
was inverted until the held arrival note was excluded from the pulse metric
(then 4–8× real), and strict restatement pinned selfsim at 1.0 until
alternate restatements got rotated (→ 0.63).

**Engine gap, probed not assumed.** Section `keyContexts` are parsed and
stored (`composer.h:275`) and `Section::active_scale_at` exists
(`structure.h:216`) with **zero callers** — one motif under sections keyed
C/G/D renders byte-identical notes (48/53/59/60), including on a figure that
crosses where the key signatures differ. So a real modulating
circle-of-fifths trip is engine work; the diatonic sequence is what is
authorable today. Spec with a 4-stage C++ plan:
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-31-passage-strategy-expansion-design.md`.
