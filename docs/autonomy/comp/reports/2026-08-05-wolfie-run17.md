## 1. #16 — the ending rule was overshooting, and the draw was not the reason

Run 16's fold measured generated endings at ratio p50 4.0 against a corpus 2.0
with 25% past corpus p95. New harness `final_rule_sweep.py` replays the `--v3`
batch loop WITHOUT rendering (same rng streams, so a batch measured there is
the batch the renderer would produce), which makes n=200 arms cheap.

Two mechanisms, measured separately:

- The v3 anchor is `max(other notes) * U[1.1, 1.6]`. A generated phrase's own
  longest note is **already median 2.1x / p95 8x its median pulse**, so that
  multiplier compounds on an anchor that is itself long.
- Grid completion **ceiled** the phrase onto the next barline, adding a
  further uniform [0,1) beats — p50 +0.25 — AFTER the draw. Decomposed, the
  v4 draw alone lands at p50 **2.00**, exactly the corpus median: the draw was
  never the problem, the barline pad was.

v4 `calib` keeps the longest-frequency Matt asked for twice and bounds the
magnitude instead: the final EQUALS the phrase max rather than exceeding it,
the alternative branch draws a corpus-histogram ratio **capped at corpus p95**
(the rule may never invent an ending longer than the corpus 95th percentile —
branch A can still exceed it, but only by matching a long note the phrase
already contains), and grid completion rounds to the NEAREST barline, floored
so the final note is never shortened below what it started with.

| n=200 | ratio p50 | ratio p95 | over corpus p95 | mean | final-is-longest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v3 `longest` | 3.00 | 11.00 | 0.170 | 4.27 | 0.785 |
| **v4 `calib`** | **2.50** | **7.00** | **0.065** | **3.09** | **0.620** |
| corpus (MTD n=1632) | 2.00 | 6.00 | 0.050 | — | 0.354 |

The `longest` / `corpus` / `old` arms are byte-identical in the same sweep —
this is a new arm, not a change to the old ones. A tuning anti-result worth
recording: raising the longest-branch probability 0.6 → 0.7 → 0.8 buys
final-is-longest 0.620 → 0.680 → 0.685, i.e. it **saturates**, because
nearest-grid rounding trims some finals back below the phrase max regardless.
0.6 stays.

`renders/markov_phrases6/` (calib) vs `renders/markov_phrases6/longest/` (v3):
same seed, same 24 phrases, only the last note differs. Batch ratio median
2.50 vs 4.00, max 19 vs 27, final-is-longest 18/24 vs 24/24. Composite is a
wash (0.825 vs 0.822) — the ears decide; the closure screen separates on the
outliers (p15 0.760 vs 0.482, p17 0.710 vs 0.509, mean 0.854 vs 0.827).
**REVIEW 13.**

## 3. #15 — passage mode: score the shape, not the note plausibility

`score_passage.py`. The backlog's own example reproduces exactly:
`pedal_mod_minor3rds_1_1`, 16 notes over 50 beats, **0.719** on the phrase
composite and **0.876** in passage mode.

Three stated criteria, weights 0.35 / 0.40 / 0.25:

- **tension** — windowed dissonance + density + register, measured from the
  start **up to the peak**. This was the one real design correction: a
  whole-passage least-squares slope reads NEGATIVE on exactly the passages
  that work (bruckner2 −0.02, pedal_chords −0.24), because they build and then
  *resolve*. What happens after the peak is what `arrival` is for.
- **arrival** — consonant final sonority, tension released into it, on a
  downbeat, held, and landing on the tonic.
- **coherence** — recurrence rewarded inside a band. A sequence is *supposed*
  to repeat one cell; the phrase `selfsim` screen punishes it for that.

Nothing is corpus-fitted — there is no passage corpus. The evidence is the
file's own `--degrade` harness: four mechanical degradations, each breaking
one stated criterion, mean delta over 4 representative passages —

| degradation | passage | phrase composite |
|---|---|---|
| shuffle_pitch | **−0.235** | −0.066 |
| flat | **−0.239** | −0.456 |
| offbeat_end | **−0.062** | −0.012 |
| retrograde | **−0.037** | +0.011 |

Across all 74 passage renders the two screens are **uncorrelated (Spearman
0.072)** and passage mode spreads 0.32–0.97 against the phrase composite's
0.66–0.93. Sanity anchors hold at both ends: top is the pedal buildups
(0.925–0.967), bottom is `sequence_static` (0.323), a deliberately static
control and the only piece whose coherence term drops.

Three things recorded rather than tuned away:

- **retrograde is the weakest catch**, and the reason is real: a
  build-and-resolve arc is near-symmetric in time, so reversal preserves the
  curve shape and often the grid alignment. What it destroys is tonal arrival,
  which is why the tonic term exists — with it, retrograde IS caught on the
  chordal passages (bruckner2 0.822 → 0.689, pedal_chords 0.955 → 0.863). It
  is not caught on `chain_chromatic_peak_0`, and that is a true statement
  about that passage: it really ends on beat 22.5, off the grid, so reversing
  it *improves* its arrival.
- **the first motion term was an artifact generator.** Raw dissonance+density
  spread is identically 0 for any monophonic passage — no verticals exist to
  be dissonant — so every melody-only passage read as static however far its
  register moved. Fixed to the combined z-curve.
- **on the one taste datapoint available, passage mode does not reproduce the
  verdict**: Matt picked `minor3rds_0` and passage mode puts `minor3rds_1`
  0.02 above it. Reported, not tuned to — one verdict is not a calibration
  set.
