## Front 2 — chain -> goal connective strategies (your three shapes)

> "Long chains of repeated figures moving up or down the scale are very common
> in connective passages, but typically there is a transformation at the end,
> prior to the arrival at the next passage."

That is the half the v1 `connective` was missing: it had the chain and no
goal. Your three examples are now three strategies sharing one skeleton,
CHAIN -> GOAL:

- `chain_chromatic_peak` — cell x5-7 ascending, chromatic rise to a held peak,
  scalar descent to a tonic cadence
- `chain_tonic_fanfare` — cell x4-6 descending, landing *exactly* on the
  tonic, then your fanfare rhythm on the arrived pitch (q e. s q q h q)
- `chain_arpeggio_fallback` — cell x2-3 ascending, arpeggio in thirds falling
  back almost-but-not-quite to the start (+1 net per cycle), 3 cycles, then a
  climactic rising cadence

**Chromaticism did not need an engine change.** `FigureUnit.accidental` is
applied as a transient shift that leaves the scale-degree cursor alone
(`composer.h:455`), so a raised passing tone is `(step 0, accidental +1)` and
the following diatonic step still lands correctly. Verified on rendered
pitches rather than assumed: the rise sounds 57-58-59-60-61-62-63 and the
cadence lands on C5. This also means the earlier "chromatic needs a Chromatic
scale" assumption was wrong — worth knowing for the wandering/modulation work.

Two defects the renders exposed, both fixed:
- the chain **amplifies** a monotone cell draw into one pitch hammered 20+
  times. `_sample_chain_cell` rejects those (composite 0.789 -> 0.926).
- "descends to a cadence" was landing on the nearest tonic *below the peak*,
  which measured **one step away**. Now at least a fifth of descent.

## Front 3 — pedal point: chords over the pedal, and the 8x problem

> "The most common pedal point is chords moving over the pedal tone,
> increasing tension via increasing dissonance ... leading to a climactic
> cadence, say a french or german 6th to a tonic ... followed by V(7) to a
> full cadence."

`pedal_chords` does this with no chord plumbing — a chord is three held
voices, and extra melodic parts already work. Diatonic triads over a dominant
pedal are ordered by **measured** tension against that pedal (no pedal pitch
class = +2; each second/seventh/tritone against it = +1), so rising dissonance
is a property of the render: tension curve `0-0-0-3-3-4` across the buildup.

The cadence, hand-voiced, verified in the rendered pitches:

| beat | chord | voices | bass |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 | Ger6 | F#4 D#4 G#3 | G2 |
| 28 | I(6/4) | G4 E4 C3 | G2 |
| 32 | V7 | F4 D4 B3 | G2 |
| 36 | I | G4 E4 C3 | **C2** |

The augmented sixth Ab-F# resolves outward onto the pedal's own G. The pedal
**releases** to the tonic for the final chord — a "full cadence" needs the
root in the bass, and held to the end the arrival never actually lands. One
correction to your description: the tonic over the held pedal is 6/4, not
first inversion, since the pedal is the fifth.

**The 8x acceleration was two separate problems.**
- Capping the level count fixed the speed but gutted the passage (9 notes,
  selfsim 1.0, composite 0.31) — and you objected to the speed, not the
  length. The density ratio now saturates at 4x with the level count intact.
- **The metric was lying.** `accel_ratio` compared level 0's mean *including*
  its long final note against the last level's mean *excluding* its held note.
  It reported 8x for a genuine 4x diminution — measured, M0 `[0.5, 0.5, 2.0]`
  vs M2 `[0.125, 0.125, 0.5]`. Made symmetric; now reads exactly 4.0. So part
  of the "8x" you reacted to was never in the audio.

Monotone guard extended here too: selfsim 0.63 -> 0.40, composite 0.73 -> 0.91.
